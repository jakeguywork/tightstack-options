TightStack.ca · home page redesign · October 2026

Three ways in. Each one starts with the stack.

All three open on the mark alone: the exploded stack draws itself, closes up, shrinks to become the dot on the i, and the name prints around it. What differs is the sheet it happens on and what the stack does afterwards.

  1. 01

    Paper

    The logo as it was drawn: navy on white, centred, with concentric hairlines inked behind it. The entry plays itself over about three and a half seconds, then the page settles into an editorial layout with large numerals, ruled lists and sticky section labels. A ruler in the left margin tracks how far down the sheet you are. Beside the list of services the stack is drawn exploded and settles one plate per kind of work as you read down. Below that the sections keep the live site’s order and copy, with the two recent sites shown as framed screenshots beside the conference copy.

    Entry: timed, about three and a half seconds, on every load.

    Open option 1
  2. 02

    Field

    The cyanotype stays: chalk on navy, left-aligned. After the name prints, the stack is the one object on the page. Scroll and it leaves the i, grows, sits beside the list of services and comes apart one plate per service as each one is read, then closes, docks as the dot on the i in the header, and drops into the name again at the foot of the page. The two recent sites appear as chalk prints.

    Entry: timed, then scroll-driven. The flying stack runs on screens wider than about 900px; phones get a still drawing.

    Open option 2
  3. 03

    Sheet

    Nothing plays by itself. The first screen is a white sheet with the exploded stack on it, and the visitor's scroll closes it, carries it to the i, prints the name, and then tucks the name into the corner as the sheet turns navy and the site begins. Scroll back up and it all reverses.

    Entry: scrubbed by scroll, about three screens' worth. The closest of the three to steven.com.

    Open option 3

Every option keeps the live page's copy, contact form, Turnstile and Worker endpoint, so any of them can replace public/index.html as is. On this preview server the form posts to nothing and Turnstile logs a hostname error; both work on tightstack.ca.

Reduced-motion visitors and visitors without JavaScript get the finished page with no entry sequence.
